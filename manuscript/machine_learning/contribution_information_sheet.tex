{\bfseries FALI: Mechanistic Decomposition of Conditional Attention-Head Importance}

\section*{1. What is the main claim, and why is it important to machine learning?}

A transformer's component importance can change after another circuit component is perturbed, but identifying that conditional change does not explain its mechanism. We introduce FALI (Factorized Attention-Level Intervention), a hypothesis-driven framework for a selected attention head that decomposes the cross-state change in its output-grounded causal usefulness into target scores ($T$), rival scores ($R$), other valid attention scores ($O$), value content ($V$), and surrounding-network context ($C$). Standard Owen attribution allocates interactions under the fixed coalition partition $\{T,R,O\}$, $\{V\}$, $\{C\}$ and reconciles the fine score decomposition with the coarse score/value/context game. The Owen mathematics is established; the contribution is the intervention-defined estimand and architecture-aligned causal game.

This distinction matters because a shared functional label can conceal different internal mechanisms. In our experiments, GPT-2 IOI and Pythia induction both exhibit positive conditional recruitment, yet their target/rival score contributions have opposite signs. Thus, detecting that a component became important is not sufficient to infer how its importance changed.

\section*{2. What precise evidence supports the claim?}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Controlled validation.} Five deterministic systems isolate $T$, $R$, $O$, $V$, and $C$ one at a time; FALI assigns the endpoint change to the manipulated channel with maximum discrepancy $1.11\times10^{-16}$. A pure $T\times C$ interaction is split symmetrically by standard Owen attribution. A randomly initialized two-layer transformer independently verifies structural masks, score-to-pattern and pattern/value-to-head-output reconstruction, direct endpoint identities, candidate-isolation, live-Q/V leakage checks, and coarse--fine reconciliation, with zero recorded model-level discrepancies at the preregistered precision.

\item \textbf{GPT-2 IOI.} On 15 canonical IOI templates crossed with BABA/ABBA patterns, four prompt seeds, and 3600 prompts, L10H2 is positively recruited after ablating the canonical primary Name Movers: conditional gain $0.298$ (95\% hierarchical CI $[0.263,0.335]$). Its FALI decomposition gives $\phi_T=0.783$ ($[0.722,0.838]$) and $\phi_R=-0.341$ ($[-0.372,-0.311]$). L10H10 provides a functional contrast: target attention increases strongly, but conditional gain is negative ($-0.263$, CI $[-0.302,-0.225]$). The four preregistered GPT-2 fine-channel hypotheses pass across the broad canonical design and the template-overlap-excluded sensitivity.

\item \textbf{Intervention checks.} The target-positive/rival-negative GPT-2 channels retain their directions under the tested matched-mean and matched-resample primary-damage schemes. We do not report a blanket robustness claim: the preregistered EF-5 experiment-level gate failed because its zero-damage reference criterion was not satisfied.

\item \textbf{Preregistered cross-setting test.} In Pythia-160M induction, a discovery procedure that was forbidden from inspecting FALI channels selected L5H0$\rightarrow$L8H2; confirmation used held-out seeds 1, 8, and 22. L8H2 is positively recruited: conditional effect $0.621$ ($[0.456,0.860]$), conditional gain $0.486$ ($[0.326,0.722]$). The preregistered GPT-2 mechanism does not transfer: $\phi_T=-0.144$ ($[-0.247,-0.084]$) and $\phi_R=+0.118$ ($[0.058,0.219]$). The EF-6 gate therefore fails specifically because both fine-score directions reverse while recruitment remains confirmed.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{3. Which papers are closest, and how is this work related?}

The closest lines of work are: (i) self-repair and backup-head studies, including the Hydra Effect, Copy Suppression, and broader self-repair analyses; (ii) Conditional Co-Ablation (CoAx), which measures which components become more important after a primary set is removed; (iii) interaction-aware attribution, especially Causal Head Gating and \emph{The Curse of Multiple Mediators}, which establish dependence of component effects on the surrounding model; (iv) activation/path patching; and (v) QK/OV and recent causal routing-versus-value decompositions.

FALI is positioned downstream of these methods. It does not claim first discovery of self-repair, conditional importance, context-dependent component roles, routing/value separation, or Shapley/Owen attribution. Given a selected attention head whose causal usefulness changes across two model states, FALI asks which architecture-level changes account for that change under an explicit counterfactual game. To our knowledge, the closest prior work does not provide this exact output-grounded cross-state decomposition into score/value/context with target/rival/other-score resolution. The manuscript states this novelty claim narrowly and immediately cites the closest conditional-attribution, interaction, and routing/value work.

\section*{4. Have parts appeared previously? What is the significant contribution beyond them?}

An earlier public preprint, \emph{When Attention Repairs Itself: A Mechanistic Study of Backup Heads in GPT-2} (May 2026), used the earlier name ``Frozen Attention Logit Intervention'' and centered an attention-based interpretation of GPT-2 backup behavior. Corrected functional analysis subsequently falsified the assumption that increased target attention implied increased causal usefulness.

The present work is a scientific reconstruction rather than a cosmetic extension. It changes the estimand from attention change to output-grounded conditional causal usefulness; replaces the earlier two-score analysis with the five-factor Factorized Attention-Level Intervention game; corrects the attribution rule to standard Owen values; adds synthetic and random-transformer ground-truth validation; uses the corrected canonical three-Name-Mover perturbation; confirms fine-score effects across broad IOI templates and multiple seeds; tests alternative damage schemes; and performs a preregistered Pythia induction transfer. That transfer rejects the proposed GPT-2 fine-score generalization by producing opposite target/rival signs. The current manuscript therefore supersedes the earlier mechanistic interpretation and explicitly reports the negative results that motivated the reconstruction.

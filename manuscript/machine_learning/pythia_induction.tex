\section{Cross-Setting Test: Pythia Induction}\label{sec:pythia}

\subsection{Preregistered transfer design}\label{sec:pythia-design}

The GPT-2 IOI results establish a stable target-positive/rival-negative fine-score pattern under the FALI game, but they do not establish that this organization transfers beyond that task and model. We therefore preregistered one cross-setting test using Pythia-160M on synthetic repeated-token induction. The transfer hypothesis was deliberately specific: if a later induction head became more causally useful after an earlier induction head was removed, we predicted the same fine-score directions observed in GPT-2, $\phi_T>0$ and $\phi_R<0$. Failure of either direction was to count against transfer rather than trigger selection of another model, task, or head pair.

The induction dataset contains a length-32 block of distinct tokens followed by a repeated prefix. For query offsets $j\in\{8,16,24\}$, the model receives $[x_0,\ldots,x_{31},x_0,\ldots,x_j]$ and is evaluated on the log probability of $x_{j+1}$ at the final query position $q=32+j$. A matched control replaces the first block with a disjoint token sequence while preserving the repeated suffix. The FALI target edge is fixed as $(q,k)$ with $k=j+1$, corresponding to the induction source position. The scalar task metric is the target-token log probability; induction advantage is the difference between repeated and control inputs.

Head selection was separated from FALI attribution. Discovery used seed 42 only, with 128 examples at each offset (384 total), and was forbidden from computing or inspecting any $\phi_T,\phi_R,\phi_O,\phi_V,\phi_C$, or $\Phi_{\mathrm{SCORE}}$ quantity. Primary heads had to exhibit positive clean induction attention and a positive intact causal effect; candidate heads had to occur later in the network and show positive conditional effect and positive conditional gain after primary ablation. Among eligible ordered pairs, the preregistered rule selected the pair with the largest balanced conditional gain. Of 241 evaluated pairs, 238 satisfied the eligibility rules; this procedure selected L5H0 as the primary head and L8H2 as the candidate. On the discovery cohort, L8H2 had conditional gain $1.684$ and conditional effect $1.804$. The selected pair was then frozen before confirmation.

Confirmation used three independent prompt-generation cohorts (seeds 1, 8, and 22), again with 128 examples at each of the three offsets, giving 384 examples per seed and 1152 held-out examples in total. Observed quantities are balanced first across offsets within seed and then equally across seeds. Confidence intervals use 10,000 deterministic bootstrap draws with resampling inside each seed--offset cell; seeds themselves are not resampled. Five gates were frozen: induction behavior (G0), causal importance of the primary head (G1), positive conditional recruitment of L8H2 after L5H0 ablation (G2), transferred $\phi_T>0$ (G3), and transferred $\phi_R<0$ (G4). The experiment-level gate required all five to confirm.

\subsection{Recruitment generalizes but the fine mechanism reverses}\label{sec:pythia-results}

The held-out Pythia cohorts satisfy the behavioral and functional prerequisites. Repeated sequences yield an induction advantage of $14.017$ log-probability units (95\% CI $[12.280,15.939]$). Ablating L5H0 produces a positive primary drop of $1.146$ ($[0.887,1.539]$), confirming that the selected primary head is causally useful for the induction metric. More importantly, L8H2 is positively recruited after that intervention: its conditional effect is $0.621$ ($[0.456,0.860]$) and its conditional gain is $0.486$ ($[0.326,0.722]$). Both quantities are positive in every confirmation seed and at every offset. Thus G0, G1, and G2 confirm independently of the FALI fine-score hypotheses.

The preregistered fine-score mechanism does not transfer. Instead of the predicted positive target contribution, FALI gives
\begin{equation}
\phi_T=-0.144,\qquad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[-0.247,-0.084],
\end{equation}
with a negative mean in all three confirmation seeds and all three offsets. Instead of the predicted negative rival contribution, FALI gives
\begin{equation}
\phi_R=+0.118,\qquad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[0.058,0.219],
\end{equation}
with a positive mean in every confirmation seed and offset. The reversal is therefore not a near-zero failure of detection. Relative to the preregistered absolute score-channel denominator $0.261807$, $|\phi_T|$ and $|\phi_R|$ account for approximately $54.9\%$ and $45.0\%$, respectively, so both channels also clear the prespecified magnitude threshold in absolute value while having the wrong signs for transfer.

The remaining fine-score channel is negligible on average ($\phi_O=-0.00027$, descriptive only). At the coarse level, the value contribution is small and negative ($\phi_V=-0.00210$), whereas the surrounding-context contribution is positive and large ($\phi_C=0.514$). Together with $\phi_T$ and $\phi_R$, these quantities sum to the observed conditional gain under Owen efficiency, up to the recorded numerical precision. We do not interpret the large context term as a uniquely localized external mechanism; as defined in Section~\ref{sec:factors}, it is the contribution assigned to the surrounding computation under the specified intervention game.

Accordingly, the preregistered EF-6 gate is \textsc{Fail}: G0, G1, and G2 confirm, while both mechanistic transfer gates G3 and G4 do not. No alternate head pair, model, threshold, or task was substituted after this outcome. This is a failed universality hypothesis, not a failed recruitment result. In both studied settings a later attention head becomes more causally useful after a primary component is perturbed, but the fine target/rival score organization is opposite: GPT-2 IOI has $\phi_T>0$ and $\phi_R<0$, whereas Pythia induction has $\phi_T<0$ and $\phi_R>0$. The result therefore argues against treating the high-level label of conditional recruitment as evidence for one shared fine-score mechanism. It does not establish that no common recruitment mechanism exists across transformers generally; the inference is restricted to the two model/task settings tested here.

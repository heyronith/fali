\section{Cross-Setting Test: Pythia Induction}\label{sec:pythia}

\subsection{Preregistered transfer design}\label{sec:pythia-design}

We preregistered one transfer test of the GPT-2 target-positive/rival-negative pattern using Pythia-160M on repeated-token induction. For offsets $j\in\{8,16,24\}$, the input is $[x_0,\ldots,x_{31},x_0,\ldots,x_j]$, the target is $x_{j+1}$ at final query $q=32+j$, and the FALI target key is $k=j+1$; a matched control replaces the first block with disjoint tokens. The transfer hypothesis predicted $\phi_T>0$ and $\phi_R<0$ for a later head that became more useful after an earlier induction head was removed. Failure of either sign counted against transfer.

Head selection was separated from FALI attribution. Discovery used seed 42 only (128 examples per offset; 384 total) and was forbidden from inspecting FALI attributions. Eligibility required a causally useful primary head and a later candidate with positive conditional effect and gain; the frozen selection rule chose L5H0 as primary and L8H2 as candidate. Confirmation then used seeds 1, 8, and 22, again with 128 examples per offset (1152 total). Estimates balance offsets within seed and seeds equally; 95\% intervals use 10,000 deterministic bootstrap draws within seed--offset cells. The five frozen gates were induction behavior (G0), primary causal importance (G1), positive conditional recruitment (G2), transferred $\phi_T>0$ (G3), and transferred $\phi_R<0$ (G4).

\subsection{Recruitment generalizes but the fine mechanism reverses}\label{sec:pythia-results}

The held-out cohorts satisfy the behavioral and functional prerequisites. Induction advantage is $14.017$ (95\% CI $[12.280,15.939]$), the L5H0 primary drop is $1.146$ ($[0.887,1.539]$), and L8H2 is positively recruited: conditional effect $0.621$ ($[0.456,0.860]$) and conditional gain $0.486$ ($[0.326,0.722]$). These quantities have the required direction in every confirmation seed and offset, so G0--G2 confirm independently of the fine-score hypotheses.

The fine mechanism reverses. FALI gives $\phi_T=-0.144$ (95\% CI $[-0.247,-0.084]$) and $\phi_R=+0.118$ ($[0.058,0.219]$), with the reversed sign in every seed and offset. This is not a near-zero failure: relative to the preregistered absolute score-channel denominator $0.261807$, $|\phi_T|$ and $|\phi_R|$ account for approximately $54.9\%$ and $45.0\%$, respectively. The remaining channels are small for $O$ ($\phi_O=-0.00027$, descriptive) and $V$ ($\phi_V=-0.00210$), while the surrounding-context contribution is positive and large ($\phi_C=0.514$); as elsewhere, $C$ is not interpreted as a uniquely localized external mechanism.

Accordingly, EF-6 is \textsc{Fail}: G0--G2 confirm, while G3 and G4 do not. No alternate pair, model, task, or threshold was substituted after this outcome. GPT-2 IOI and Pythia induction therefore both contain positive conditional recruitment in the studied cases, but their fine target/rival organizations are opposite. This falsifies transfer of the preregistered GPT-2 fine-score mechanism to the tested Pythia setting; it does not establish that no shared recruitment mechanism exists more generally.

\section{Method Validation}\label{sec:validation}

Before applying FALI to pretrained models, we tested whether the implemented intervention game recovers known factors in controlled systems and whether its tensor-level interventions reproduce ordinary transformer computation at the two natural endpoints. These tests were preregistered as implementation and falsification checks rather than empirical evidence about any pretrained model.

\subsection{Analytical and synthetic ground truth}\label{sec:synthetic}

We first constructed deterministic attention systems in which the intact and perturbed states differed in exactly one FALI factor at a time. Each system included a causal score tensor, masked softmax, value vectors, attention output $z=P V$, and a context-specific downstream readout. We evaluated all 32 FALI cells and computed the standard Owen values under the frozen SCORE/VALUE/CONTEXT coalition structure.

In the five single-channel cases, only the intended factor received nonzero attribution. The target-score case produced $\Delta_h=0.268952$ with $\phi_T=0.268952$ and all other channels exactly zero at recorded precision; the rival-score case produced $\Delta_h=-0.494100$ with only $\phi_R$ nonzero. The corresponding $O$, $V$, and $C$ constructions likewise assigned the complete endpoint change to the manipulated channel. Across these cases, the largest discrepancy between the intended contribution and the recovered attribution was $1.11\times10^{-16}$.

A separate construction tested a pure cross-factor interaction rather than an additive single-channel effect. We designed a $T\times C$ case with total endpoint change $\Delta_h\approx2$ and no marginal effect from either factor in isolation. Standard Owen attribution split this interaction symmetrically: $\phi_T\approx1$ and $\phi_C\approx1$, with $R$, $O$, and $V$ equal to zero. This test is important because an earlier fixed-block-order formulation would have assigned the interaction asymmetrically; the final method instead uses standard Owen attribution over all admissible block orders.

The synthetic systems also verified the endpoint and coarse--fine identities. All-$A$ and all-$B$ hybrid scores, patterns, and head outputs reconstructed their cached endpoints with zero recorded error. The endpoint difference matched the independently constructed conditional gain exactly. Collapsing $T,R,O$ into SCORE and comparing the resulting three-player SCORE/VALUE/CONTEXT game with the corresponding coarse factorial decomposition gave a maximum discrepancy of $1.11\times10^{-16}$.

\subsection{Transformer implementation invariants}\label{sec:implementation-validation}

We next repeated the mechanical checks in a randomly initialized two-layer TransformerLens model rather than a hand-constructed attention system. The model had two attention heads per layer, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=32$, $d_{\mathrm{head}}=16$, $d_{\mathrm{mlp}}=64$, context length 8, and vocabulary size 48. A layer-0 head served as the primary perturbed component and a layer-1 head as the FALI candidate. The model was evaluated in FP32 on CPU with deterministic seed 4242; it was not pretrained and these results are not treated as evidence about learned transformer mechanisms.

The implementation first had to reproduce the model's own candidate computation. Applying masked softmax to the cached attention scores reconstructed both intact and perturbed attention patterns with zero recorded error, and multiplying those patterns by the cached value tensors reconstructed the candidate $z$ tensors with zero recorded error. Masked coordinates received zero probability. Batch-specific partitions were also checked with different target keys across examples; every example had exactly one target coordinate, zero overlap among $T$, $R$, and $O$, and complete coverage of structurally valid score coordinates.

We then compared the two complete FALI endpoints against four independent ordinary forward passes: candidate present and candidate zeroed in the intact context, and candidate present and candidate zeroed in the perturbed context. The direct intact effect was $0.455046$, the direct conditional effect was $-0.085684$, and their difference was $-0.540729$. The corresponding FALI endpoint cells reproduced all three quantities with zero recorded discrepancy. The injected all-$A$ and all-$B$ candidate outputs also matched the independently cached candidate outputs exactly.

Finally, we tested intervention isolation. Overwriting the candidate head left every non-candidate head unchanged at the injection site. Deliberately applying large perturbations to the candidate's live query computation, value computation, or both had no effect on the final metric once the full candidate $z$ was overwritten; all three leakage errors were zero at recorded precision. The fine FALI game and its collapsed SCORE/VALUE/CONTEXT quotient also agreed exactly in this model-level test.

These checks establish a limited but necessary claim: the implemented FALI intervention pipeline behaves as specified in controlled systems, including factor isolation, interaction allocation, structural masking, endpoint recovery, and coarse--fine reconciliation. They do not establish that the chosen factorization is unique, that mixed hybrid states are on-manifold, or that a large attribution identifies a unique natural causal pathway. Those interpretation limits remain part of the method and are revisited in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
